The traveler stopped at the edge of the road. The evening light fell sideways across the hills and stained them a copper that seemed to remember something old.

—Heading north? —asked the old man, without looking up from the fire.

—As far as the road takes me.

The old man nodded the way someone nods at an answer they have heard a thousand times. He took something from his pocket and set it on the stone between them: a bronze coin, worn smooth, marked with a symbol the traveler could not read.

When the seventh moon crosses the threshold, the broken sword shall sing again.

—You'll need it —the old man said—. More than you think.

The traveler closed his hand around the coin and felt the cold of the metal climb his wrist like a current. He asked nothing else. He didn't have to. The road went on toward the hills and the wind carried the smell of distant smoke.
