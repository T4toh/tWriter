The tower had fallen, but not entirely. There was still an arch, a sunken stone staircase, and a round room the wind had emptied of anything fragile. What the wind hadn't taken, the years had.

The traveler went in first. Liriel stayed outside, watching the slope they had climbed, like someone who still hadn't decided whether to trust the road.

—Don't touch anything with the coin hand —she said from behind.

—And if I want to find out what happens anyway?

—Don't complain later.

The floor was covered in grey dust, too fine a grey to be only dust. In the center, almost buried, lay a short, notched piece of metal. He picked it up with the hand that did not carry the coin. The piece was heavy, cold, and the light coming through the broken roof reflected on the edge in a way that felt strange, as if it remembered being larger.
