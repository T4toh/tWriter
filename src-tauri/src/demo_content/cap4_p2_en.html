The woman led them to the far end of the burned village, where a dry well opened in the ground like a mouth. They climbed down a wooden ladder into a small chamber with hardened mud walls and a low table at the center. On the table, wrapped in black cloth, lay the second fragment.

—You don't take without giving —the woman said—. That is the only rule. If you take it, you have to leave something in exchange. Something that matters to you.

—And if I don't?

—Then what happened to them happens. —She gestured upward, toward the burned village—. They tried to take it without paying.

The traveler looked at the fragment. It was smaller than the first, but the shape felt close, as if they were pieces that carried a memory of how they fit together. He felt the weight of the coin and the first fragment in his bag, both warming slightly, both waiting.

—And what did you leave? —he asked the woman, without looking at her.

—What I had. —And that was all she said.
