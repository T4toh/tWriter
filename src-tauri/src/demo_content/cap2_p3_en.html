They found shelter in a cleft of the mountain, deep enough to keep out the wind and dry enough for a fire. The woman gathered branches with an efficiency that does not come quickly.

—Your name. —She said it as a statement.

—Later.

—Fair. —She lit the fire with two clean strokes—. Mine is Liriel. I don't know if it helps you, but there it is.

Later, with night settled over them, Liriel showed him how to light a flame without a spark. It wasn't exactly magic, she said. It was remembering something the body already knew. The traveler tried five times and on the sixth the air in front of his hand grew warm — barely — a red point, weak, that shivered and went out.

—That. —Liriel smiled for the first time—. That's already a flame.

—It's not enough for anything.

—Everything starts like that. Everything that ends up burning a lot, started like that.
