The first village appeared at noon, at the bottom of a long, narrow valley. The houses were pale stone, the roofs dark thatch. The air smelled of fresh bread and wet wool.

A woman was carrying a bucket from the well. She looked at him the way one looks at an unfamiliar horse: without fear, without trust, measuring.

—Looking for someone? —she said, setting the bucket down.

—Looking for the road north.

—The road north has been cut for three weeks. —She paused, as if the sentence were longer inside her than what she'd said out loud—. There was a fire in the forest, and no one came back.

The traveler nodded. He felt the old man's coin grow warm against his leg. The woman saw he was going to enter the forest anyway and sighed without surprise.

—Then at least eat something before you die —she said, and gestured for him to follow.
