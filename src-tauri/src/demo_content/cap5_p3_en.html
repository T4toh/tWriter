He went back down the slope with the sword wrapped in his cloak. Liriel was waiting on the ledge, seated, her face into the wind. She did not stand up when she saw him appear.

—You came back —she said, without surprise but with something close to relief.

—I came back.

—And the sword?

—Here.

She looked at it through the cloak without asking for it. She nodded. The traveler sat down beside her and they looked together at the valley, far below, where the smoke of the living villages could barely be seen and, further south, the road by which he had come to the forest a lifetime ago.

—And now? —he asked.

—Now comes the hard part —Liriel said—. Knowing what to do with it.

The traveler did not answer. The afternoon was falling on the hills with a copper he already knew, a copper that seemed to remember something old. He felt, once again, that the road had chosen him and not the other way around, and for the first time in a long time that didn't seem like a bad thing.
