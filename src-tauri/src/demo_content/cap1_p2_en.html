He walked until the sun was gone and the sky turned into a bowl full of cold stars. The old man's coin sat heavy in his pocket, as if it carried a memory of its own.

He thought of his mother. Of the low voice she used for the stories she told him before sleep, stories of kingdoms that no longer existed and of men who walked away and never came back.

He built a fire at the foot of a dry tree. The night was still, but not at peace: it was the stillness that comes before important decisions.

—What if I don't come back? —he said aloud, expecting no answer.

The fire cracked, and that was answer enough. The traveler leaned his head against the trunk and watched the embers. Somewhere in the hills, far off, an animal howled once and then went quiet. He fell asleep thinking of the coin and of the worn symbol, not knowing yet that the road north had already chosen him.
