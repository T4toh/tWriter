The traveler set the bronze coin on the table. The coin tapped against the wood and lay still, dull, as if it knew that its work was finished.

—Is it enough? —he asked.

—It was what it was —the woman said—. And that is always enough, when it was real.

When he lifted the fragment, his bag weighed more, but not as a physical weight: it weighed the way decisions weigh when you make them in earnest.

They climbed back to the edge of the well and walked through the burned village without speaking. Liriel walked a step behind, watching the sides. When they reached the road, dusk was falling again, and the traveler's feet felt heavier than his boots.

—You're missing one —Liriel said—. And the last one always costs more than all the others combined.

—That's what people who didn't go to the last one say.

—It's also what the ones who came back say. —She held his gaze a second longer than necessary—. There must be a reason.
