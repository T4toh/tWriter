He crossed the ridge. On the other side, on a flat stone, the third fragment waited for him. There was no guardian, no trap, nothing but the wind rising from the drop and a silence that hurt. The traveler suddenly understood that this was the worst of the trials: having no one against him.

He approached slowly. He picked up the fragment.

The three pieces grew warm at the same time, drawn together like animals that recognize each other. The traveler set them on the stone without thinking. They fit without a single sound. The sword was whole, cold, dull, and then, at some moment he could not measure, it stopped being cold.

—Not to use you —the traveler said, holding it with both hands—. To give you back.

The sword did not answer. It did not need to. He felt in his arms the exact weight he had expected, and knew, without thinking, that this weight was going to be his forever, even if the sword changed hands again.
