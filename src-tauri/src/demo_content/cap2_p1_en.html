The Shadow Forest was not dark, the way people said. It was, instead, too green, a deep and taut green that seemed to cover something that did not want to be seen.

The traveler had been walking for two hours when he heard a voice behind him.

—That road doesn't lead anywhere worth going.

He turned slowly. A woman was leaning against a tree, wearing a cloak the color of moss. He had not heard her coming, and that already said quite a bit.

—Who are you? —he asked, his hand near his knife.

—Someone who came from the other side of the forest and is still alive. —She smiled without warmth—. That should be reference enough.

The traveler studied her. She was young, but she carried the stillness of people who have already taken several trips toward uncertain destinations. On the palm of her left hand she had a tattoo he recognized at once: the same worn symbol of the bronze coin.

—Did the old man by the river give it to you? —she asked, watching his eyes.
